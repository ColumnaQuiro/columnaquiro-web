## What is a cervicogenic headache?

A cervicogenic headache originates in the structures of the neck — the joints, muscles, and nerves of the cervical spine — but is felt as head pain, usually on one side. Unlike migraine, its source isn't the brain itself but the neck.

## Most common causes

Sustained poor posture at a computer or on a phone, muscle tension from stress, previous neck injuries or whiplash, and reduced mobility in the cervical vertebrae are the most frequent causes. Sleeping in awkward positions or with an unsuitable pillow can also play a role.

## How to tell it apart from other headaches

It usually starts at the base of the skull and spreads toward the forehead or eye area, gets worse with neck movement or prolonged postures, and eases when you change position or apply heat. It doesn't typically come with intense nausea or aura, as migraine often does.

## How chiropractic care can help

A chiropractor assesses the mobility of the cervical vertebrae and, through gentle adjustments, mobilization techniques, and postural guidance, helps reduce the tension driving the pain. Many patients notice gradual improvement as neck mobility is restored.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a cervicogenic headache dangerous?**
Usually not, but other causes should be ruled out if the pain is very intense, sudden, or comes with other neurological symptoms.

**How long does it take to improve with chiropractic care?**
It varies, but many people notice relief within the first few sessions, though full recovery of mobility can take several weeks.

**Can this type of headache be prevented?**
Yes — paying attention to posture at work, taking active breaks, and doing gentle daily neck stretches all help a lot.

**When should I see a professional?**
If the headache is frequent, intense, or interferes with your daily life, it's a good time to get evaluated and rule out other causes.
