A **chiropractor** assesses and treats problems of the musculoskeletal system, especially the **spine**: back pain, neck pain, some headaches and joint aches. Their best-known tool is the **adjustment**, a quick, precise hand movement on a stiff joint, but a good session also includes examination, other hands-on techniques, exercises and advice for your daily life.

## What happens at the first visit

1. **Case history**: what's wrong, since when, what makes it worse or better, your medical history and medication.
2. **Screening for warning signs**: questions and tests to rule out anything that shouldn't be treated by hand and needs a doctor.
3. **Examination**: posture, movement of the spine and joints, palpation, strength, sensation and reflexes.
4. **Explanation**: what we think is going on, what treatment can and can't do, and how long we expect it to take.
5. **First treatment**, if everything fits.

At our clinic, the first visit lasts about an hour. We go through it in detail in [your first chiropractic visit: what to expect](/en/blog/first-chiropractic-visit-what-to-expect/).

## What happens at a follow-up session

Between 15 and 20 minutes: we review how you've been, re-examine the treated areas, adjust where needed, go over your exercises and progress them. If there's no improvement after a few sessions, the approach is rethought or you're referred.

## Techniques chiropractors use

- **Adjustment or spinal manipulation**: a quick, small-amplitude thrust on a specific joint. Sometimes there's a "crack", which is gas released in the joint, not a bone "going back into place".
- **Mobilisation**: slower, gentler movements without a thrust. Used when an adjustment isn't appropriate or you'd rather not have one.
- **Soft tissue work**: pressure on trigger points, myofascial release, assisted stretching.
- **Therapeutic exercise**: mobility and strength work for home, which is what keeps the results.
- **Advice**: workstation, sleep, physical activity.

## What chiropractors treat

Where the evidence is strongest:

- **Low back pain**, acute and chronic.
- **Neck pain**.
- **Cervicogenic headache** (the kind that starts at the back of the neck) and some tension headaches.

Other common reasons people come in: sciatica, pain between the shoulder blades, shoulder, hip or knee aches related to how you move, and back pain in pregnancy.

## What they don't treat

- Conditions outside the musculoskeletal system: digestive, respiratory, blood pressure, immune.
- **Fractures**, infections or tumours.
- Neurological or vascular emergencies.

A good chiropractor tells you this plainly and refers you when something doesn't fit.

## Benefits you can expect

- **Less pain** and **more movement**, usually within the first few weeks.
- **Getting back to what you do**: working, sleeping, training with less discomfort.
- A **home plan** that reduces relapses.

What you shouldn't expect: to have your spine "put back" for good, or to cure illnesses. We go through the evidence in [chiropractic pros and cons](/en/blog/chiropractic-in-valencia-benefits-considerations/).

## Chiropractor, physiotherapist or osteopath

All three treat musculoskeletal pain with their hands and exercise, and for back pain the results are similar. Chiropractors focus more on the spine and the adjustment; physiotherapists on exercise and rehabilitation of the whole body, and physiotherapy is a regulated health profession in Spain; osteopaths use a wider mix of manual techniques. We compare them in [chiropractor or physiotherapist?](/en/blog/chiropractor-or-physiotherapist/) and [chiropractors vs osteopaths](/en/blog/difference-chiropractors-vs-osteopaths/).

## Training

In Spain chiropractic isn't a regulated health profession. A fully trained chiropractor holds a **five- or six-year university chiropractic degree** obtained outside Spain, and is usually a member of the **Spanish Chiropractic Association (AEQ)**. More in [how to choose a chiropractor in Spain](/en/blog/how-to-choose-a-chiropractor-in-spain/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Does an adjustment hurt?**
It shouldn't. There may be brief pressure and some stiffness the next day, especially after the first sessions.

**How long is a session?**
At our clinic, the first is about an hour and follow-ups are 15-20 minutes.

**Do I need X-rays?**
Only if the examination or your history call for them. They aren't requested routinely.

**How many sessions will I need?**
It depends on the problem. Many people notice a change within 3-6 sessions. See [how many chiropractic sessions do I need?](/en/blog/how-many-chiropractic-sessions-do-i-need/).

**Do I need a referral from my doctor?**
No, you can book directly. If the assessment shows something that needs your doctor, we refer you.
