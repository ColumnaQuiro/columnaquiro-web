## Vertigo can have many causes

Vertigo is a spinning or unbalanced sensation that can originate in the inner ear, the central nervous system, circulatory issues, or, in some cases, the neck. That's why it's important to first rule out causes such as inner-ear conditions with a medical professional, since treatment varies greatly depending on the origin.

## What is cervicogenic vertigo?

When the origin is in the neck, we call it cervicogenic vertigo. Restrictions in the joints and tension in the upper cervical area can alter the information the body receives about its position in space, causing a feeling of dizziness or instability, especially when turning the head.

## Symptoms that often go along with it

Besides dizziness or a feeling of instability, it's common to see neck stiffness, pain at the base of the skull, and symptoms that intensify with certain neck movements rather than with changes in the position of the whole body.

## How chiropractic care helps

When a cervical component is confirmed, gentle chiropractic adjustments aim to improve the mobility of the upper neck vertebrae and reduce the associated muscle tension. Many people notice a decrease in how often and how intensely episodes occur when this is combined with cervical proprioception exercises.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can all vertigo be treated with chiropractic care?**
No. Only vertigo with a cervical component tends to respond to this approach, which is why it's essential to first rule out other causes with your doctor.

**How do I know if my vertigo has a cervical origin?**
If it comes with neck pain or stiffness and worsens when turning or moving the head, it may have a cervical component, though a professional evaluation is essential.

**Is it safe to receive cervical adjustments if I have vertigo?**
We carry out a careful assessment before any adjustment, and only gentle, adapted techniques are used when appropriate.

**How long does it take to notice improvement?**
It depends on each case, but some patients notice changes within the first few weeks of consistent care.

**When should I see a medical professional?**
Whenever vertigo is severe, sudden, or comes with hearing loss, double vision, or difficulty speaking, seek medical attention immediately.
