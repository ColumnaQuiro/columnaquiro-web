## Why the backpack matters for their back

Children's spines are still developing, and carrying weight the wrong way over the years can encourage poor posture and muscle overload. Choosing the right school backpack is a simple way to protect their back starting now.

## The right weight

As a general guideline, a loaded backpack shouldn't exceed roughly 10% to 15% of the child's body weight. Checking the contents daily and removing anything not needed that day goes a long way toward staying within that limit.

## How it should fit

Wide, padded straps, a well-cushioned back panel, and, whenever possible, a hip belt distribute the weight better. The backpack should sit close to the back, with the top at shoulder height and never hanging below the waist.

## Habits that also help

Teaching your child to wear both straps (never sling it over one shoulder), packing heavier items closer to the back, and encouraging breaks and stretches if the walk to school is long are simple habits that add up.

## Frequently asked questions

**Rolling backpack or backpack straps?**
Both can work well; what matters most is that the total weight is appropriate and, if it's worn on the back, that it's properly adjusted.

**At what age should I start paying attention to this?**
From the moment school starts, since postural habits form from an early age.

**How do I know if the backpack is too heavy?**
If your child leans forward while wearing it, struggles to put it on alone, or complains of back or shoulder pain, it's probably too heavy.

**Can chiropractic care help in these cases?**
Yes, especially if there's already discomfort or associated tension; a postural check can catch imbalances early.

**When should I see a professional?**
If you notice recurring back pain, shoulder asymmetry, or changes in your child's posture, it's worth having them evaluated.
