## Why is crawling so important?

Crawling is much more than a way of getting around: it's one of a baby's first experiences coordinating arms and legs in a cross pattern, which stimulates communication between both brain hemispheres and lays the groundwork for later skills such as writing or fine motor coordination.

## Motor and sensory benefits

Crawling strengthens the muscles of the trunk, shoulders, and hips, improves balance, and develops proprioception, that is, the baby's ability to sense where their body is in space. It also supports visual development, since the baby learns to focus on objects at different distances while moving.

## What to do if my baby doesn't crawl much

Some babies go straight from sitting to standing, barely crawling at all. While this isn't automatically a cause for alarm, it can help to offer more tummy-time opportunities and floor exploration before they start walking, since this stage brings benefits that other forms of movement don't cover in quite the same way.

## How to encourage crawling at home

Setting aside daily tummy time from the first few months, placing toys slightly out of reach to motivate movement, and leaving safe floor space for exploring, are simple ways to encourage a baby to crawl naturally.

## Frequently asked questions

**At what age do babies usually start crawling?**
It usually happens between 7 and 10 months, although every baby has their own developmental pace.

**Is it a problem if my baby never crawls?**
Not always, but it's worth mentioning to their pediatrician to assess motor development together.

**Is crawling linked to language or learning?**
It's thought that the cross-body coordination involved in crawling supports the neurological development related to these skills, though it's not the only factor.

**Can I help my baby learn to crawl?**
Yes, by offering tummy time, safe space, and things that encourage them to move toward them.

**When should I see a professional?**
If you notice clear asymmetries in movement, stiffness, or motor development that's very different from what's expected for their age.
