## The connection between the neck and the ear

The neck and the ear are more connected than most people realize. The upper cervical spine shares nerve and muscle structures with the balance system, so a problem in the upper neck vertebrae can produce symptoms that are felt in the ear.

## Symptoms that can appear

Common ones include a feeling of pressure or fullness in the ear, dizziness or unsteadiness when turning the head, and in some cases ringing (tinnitus) that worsens with neck tension. These symptoms can also have other causes, so a full assessment is important.

## Why does this happen?

Muscle tension in the upper neck and restricted movement in the upper cervical vertebrae can alter the information the brain receives about head position, which in some people translates into balance- or hearing-related symptoms.

## How chiropractic care can help

A detailed cervical examination helps identify movement restrictions in the upper neck. Targeted adjustments, along with cervical mobility exercises, can help reduce these symptoms when the underlying cause is mechanical and neck-related.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does all dizziness come from the neck?**
No. Dizziness can have many different causes, so it's always worth ruling out other origins with the appropriate specialist.

**How long before I notice improvement?**
When the cause is cervical, some patients notice changes within a few sessions, though it varies by case.

**Does tinnitus always improve with chiropractic care?**
Not always — it depends on the cause. In cases with a clear cervical component, some patients do experience relief.

**When should I see an ENT specialist?**
If symptoms are severe, persistent, or come with hearing loss, it's best to rule out ear-specific causes before attributing them to the neck.
