**Coxarthrosis** is **osteoarthritis of the hip**: wear of the cartilage covering the head of the femur and the socket of the pelvis. It causes **groin** pain and stiffness, especially when you start moving. It's very common from 50 onwards, and **exercise is the first treatment**: in most cases it keeps you active for years before a hip replacement is considered, if one is ever needed.

## What your report means

- **Right or left coxarthrosis**: osteoarthritis in that hip.
- **Bilateral coxarthrosis**: in both hips. It's common, and it doesn't usually progress at the same pace in each.
- **Grade** (mild, moderate, severe, or grades 1 to 4): describes how much joint space has been lost on the X-ray. X-ray and pain don't always match: some people have a lot of wear and little pain, and vice versa.
- **Coxopathy**: a general term for a hip problem, often used for osteoarthritis.

## Symptoms

- **Groin pain**, which can run down the front of the thigh to the **knee**. Sometimes the knee hurts more than the hip.
- **Stiffness when starting** to move in the morning or after sitting, easing within a few minutes.
- Difficulty **putting on socks**, cutting your toenails or crossing your legs.
- Pain **walking long distances**, with a limp in advanced stages.
- Less range of movement, especially turning the hip inwards.

Pain on the outside of the hip when lying on that side usually comes from the buttock tendons, not osteoarthritis.

## Causes and risk factors

- **Age** and **genetics**.
- **Hip shape**: dysplasia (a shallow socket) or femoroacetabular impingement from a young age.
- **Previous hip injuries**.
- **Excess weight**.
- Heavy manual work or high-impact sport over many years.

## Treatment without surgery

- **Exercise**: strengthening the buttocks and thighs and keeping mobility reduces pain and improves function. Guidelines recommend it for everyone with hip osteoarthritis.
- **Walking, swimming and stationary cycling**: activities that load the hip without heavy impact.
- **Losing weight** if needed.
- **Manual therapy** alongside exercise: in a trial of people with hip osteoarthritis, manual therapy improved pain and mobility in the short term more than exercise alone (Hoeksma et al., *Arthritis Care & Research*, 2004).
- **A walking stick in the opposite hand** to the painful hip, if walking becomes hard.
- **Pain relief** prescribed by your doctor, and sometimes injections.

## When a hip replacement is considered

When pain seriously limits your life (walking, sleeping, working) despite good conservative care. Hip replacement is one of the most successful operations. The decision is made with your orthopaedic surgeon, based on how it affects you, not just the X-ray.

## When to see a doctor

- **Sudden, severe** hip pain, or not being able to bear weight after a fall (possible fracture, especially in older people).
- **Fever** with hip pain.
- Pain that wakes you at night and **doesn't change with position**.
- Severe groin pain in a young adult with no clear cause.
- Rapid worsening over a few weeks.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is bilateral coxarthrosis?**
Osteoarthritis in both hips. Treatment is the same; one hip often hurts more than the other.

**Can coxarthrosis be cured?**
Cartilage doesn't regrow, but pain and function can improve a lot with exercise and weight control. If it becomes severe, a hip replacement solves it.

**Is walking good for coxarthrosis?**
Yes, at a pace and distance you can tolerate. Several short walks are better than one long one if that leaves you sore.

**Why does my knee hurt if the arthritis is in my hip?**
Because hip pain can travel down the thigh to the knee. If your knee hurts and the knee examination is normal, the hip needs checking.

**Does chiropractic cure coxarthrosis?**
No. It can help with pain and mobility as part of care that includes exercise and medical follow-up.
