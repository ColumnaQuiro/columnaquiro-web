## What is cervical lordosis?

Cervical lordosis is the natural, C-shaped curve formed by the seven vertebrae in the neck. This curve isn't an anatomical accident: it absorbs impact, helps distribute the weight of the head, and protects the spinal cord and nerve structures that run through that area.

## When it becomes a problem

Issues arise when that curve flattens out (a condition often called rectification) or becomes exaggerated (hyperlordosis). Sustained poor posture in front of screens, sleeping in awkward positions, trauma such as whiplash, or simply the passage of time can all alter this curve over the years.

## Common symptoms

When the cervical curve changes, it's common to notice neck stiffness and pain, headaches that start at the base of the skull, tension in the shoulders and, in some cases, tingling running down the arms. Many people also describe fatigue from holding the head upright for long periods.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can cervical lordosis be fully corrected?**
It depends on how advanced the change is and how long it's been present. In many cases, patients see a noticeable improvement in the curve and, above all, in their symptoms.

**Is this the same as cervical rectification?**
Not exactly. Rectification refers to the partial or total loss of that curve, while cervical lordosis is the name of the curve itself, which can be reduced, normal, or increased.

**How long does it take to notice improvement?**
It varies from person to person, but many patients feel relief from tension and pain within the first few weeks of consistent chiropractic care.

**Is there anything I can do at home to help?**
Yes: paying attention to screen height, avoiding long periods looking down at your phone with a flexed neck, and taking active breaks every hour make a big difference.

**When should I see a professional?**
If neck pain is persistent, comes with tingling in the arms, or is accompanied by frequent headaches, it's best to get evaluated as soon as possible.
