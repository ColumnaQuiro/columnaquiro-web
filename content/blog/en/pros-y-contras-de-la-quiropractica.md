**Chiropractic works for some things and not for others.** For mechanical back and neck pain, the evidence shows a modest benefit, similar to other recommended treatments such as exercise or physiotherapy. For conditions outside the musculoskeletal system, the evidence is thin. As a chiropractor, I think putting it this way helps you more than promising miracles.

## Pros

- **It works for back pain.** In acute low back pain, spinal manipulation is linked to modest improvements in pain and function (Paige et al., *JAMA*, 2017). In chronic low back pain, its effects are similar to those of other recommended treatments (Rubinstein et al., *BMJ*, 2019). Clinical guidelines include it among the options, alongside exercise.
- **The neck too**, and some headaches that come from the neck, combined with exercise.
- **No drugs or surgery.** It's a conservative place to start, and compatible with your doctor's treatment.
- **Time and assessment.** A first visit includes a full history and examination, which many people value.
- **It leaves you with tools**: a good plan includes exercises and changes to your day, not just adjustments.

## Cons

- **It isn't for everything.** For problems outside the musculoskeletal system (digestive, respiratory, blood pressure, immune), the evidence is thin or negative. Be wary of anyone who promises to cure them.
- **Mild side effects are common.** Stiffness or local soreness for a day or two after the first sessions is normal.
- **Serious risks are very rare but real**, mainly with neck manipulation. We go through the numbers in [is chiropractic dangerous?](/en/blog/is-chiropractic-dangerous-side-effects/).
- **It isn't regulated in Spain** as a health profession, so anyone can call themselves a chiropractor. You have to check their training.
- **The public health system doesn't cover it**, and few insurers do. See [does Spanish health insurance cover chiropractic?](/en/blog/does-spanish-health-insurance-cover-chiropractic/).
- **Oversized treatment plans.** Some clinics propose dozens of sessions up front or routine X-rays. That's rarely needed.

## Who is it a good fit for?

**A good option** if you have mechanical back or neck pain (it changes with posture and movement), stiffness, headaches that start at the base of the skull or work-posture aches, and no contraindications.

**Not the first option**, or see your doctor first, if you have:

- A recent **fracture**, advanced **osteoporosis**, a tumour or an infection in the spine.
- Serious neurological symptoms: **weakness** that's getting worse, loss of bladder or bowel control.
- A **sudden, severe neck pain or headache unlike any before**, especially with dizziness, double vision or trouble speaking.
- **Blood thinners** or recent spinal surgery: not ruled out, but it changes the techniques.

## How to tell if a chiropractor is good

- A **university chiropractic degree** of five or six years full-time, and membership of the **Spanish Chiropractic Association (AEQ)**, which requires it.
- They take a **full history and examination** before treating.
- They explain what's going on, what they can and can't do, and **how long** they expect it to take.
- They **refer you** to a doctor when something doesn't fit.
- They don't ask for routine X-rays or sign you up for long packages on day one.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does chiropractic really work?**
For mechanical back and neck pain, yes, with a modest benefit similar to other recommended treatments. For other conditions, there's no good evidence.

**Is chiropractic good or bad?**
Neither, in general: it depends on what you use it for and who treats you. Used for the right problem by a properly trained practitioner, it's a safe and useful option.

**Is it better than physiotherapy?**
For back pain, the results are similar. We compare them in [chiropractor or physiotherapist?](/en/blog/chiropractor-or-physiotherapist/).

**How many sessions will I need?**
Many people notice a change within 3-6 sessions. If there's no improvement at all after a few, the approach should be rethought. See [how many chiropractic sessions do I need?](/en/blog/how-many-chiropractic-sessions-do-i-need/).

**Do adjustments hurt?**
They shouldn't. There may be brief pressure and some stiffness the next day.
