**Yes, carpal tunnel can cause pain above the wrist**, up the forearm and sometimes as far as the shoulder. In a study of 159 people with confirmed carpal tunnel syndrome, more than a third had pain above the wrist (Stevens et al., *Muscle & Nerve*, 1999). But if your shoulder or neck hurts **and** your fingers go numb, it's worth ruling out the neck, because an irritated nerve root there causes very similar symptoms and is treated differently.

## What carpal tunnel syndrome is

It's compression of the **median nerve** as it passes through the wrist, in a narrow channel formed by the wrist bones and a ligament. That nerve supplies feeling to the thumb, index, middle finger and half of the ring finger.

## Typical symptoms

- **Tingling or numbness** in the thumb, index, middle and part of the ring finger. The little finger is usually spared.
- Worse **at night** or on waking, and better when you **shake your hand**.
- Comes on when driving, holding your phone or reading with a book in your hand.
- **Clumsiness**: dropping things, trouble with buttons.
- In advanced cases, the muscle at the base of the thumb looks thinner.

## Why can it hurt up to the shoulder?

There are three possible explanations, and telling them apart is what matters:

1. **Referred pain from the carpal tunnel itself.** The irritated nerve at the wrist can cause vague pain that travels up the forearm, the elbow and, less often, the shoulder. The tingling, though, stays in the fingers.
2. **The source is the neck.** An irritated nerve root at C6 or C7, for example from a [cervical disc protrusion](/en/blog/cervical-disc-protrusion/), causes neck and shoulder pain that travels down the arm to the same fingers.
3. **Both at once.** A nerve squeezed in the neck seems to become more sensitive to compression at the wrist, and having both problems isn't unusual.

## Wrist or neck? Clues to tell them apart

**More likely the wrist** if:

- Symptoms are worse at night and with your wrist bent.
- Shaking your hand helps.
- Your neck moves well and moving it changes nothing in your hand.

**More likely the neck** if:

- There's **neck pain** or pain between the shoulder blades.
- Turning or tilting your head back **reproduces the pain or tingling** in your arm.
- The arm pain **eases when you rest your hand on top of your head**.
- Symptoms also reach the **back of the hand** or the outer forearm.

The test that confirms it is a **nerve conduction study**, which your doctor can request.

## Causes and risk factors

- **Repeated** or sustained wrist movements: keyboard and mouse, vibrating tools, manual work.
- **Pregnancy**, because of fluid retention. It usually improves after birth.
- **Diabetes**, **hypothyroidism** and **rheumatoid arthritis**.
- **Excess weight**.
- It's more common in **women** and from the age of 40-50.

## What helps

For mild and moderate cases, treatment starts without surgery:

- A **night splint** that keeps the wrist straight. It's the simplest measure and the first one recommended.
- **Change how you use your hands**: straight wrist when typing, a vertical mouse or one at elbow height, breaks every 30-45 minutes.
- Gentle **nerve and tendon gliding exercises**, without triggering tingling.
- A **steroid injection**, prescribed by a doctor, for short-term relief.
- **Surgery** if there's weakness, thumb muscle wasting or symptoms that don't improve: it's a simple operation with good results.

## When to see a doctor

- **Weakness** in the hand or thumb.
- The muscle at the base of the thumb **thinner** than on the other hand.
- **Constant numbness** that never goes away.
- Symptoms in **both hands** with clumsiness or unsteadiness walking.
- Left shoulder pain with **chest tightness or breathlessness**: call 112.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can carpal tunnel affect the shoulder?**
It can cause pain that reaches the shoulder, but it doesn't damage the shoulder. If your shoulder hurts a lot, have your neck and the shoulder itself checked too.

**Why is it worse at night?**
We tend to bend our wrists in our sleep, which raises the pressure inside the tunnel. That's why a night splint helps.

**Does it go away on its own?**
Sometimes, especially in pregnancy. If it lasts months or is getting worse, it's worth treating to avoid nerve damage.

**Will any wrist support do?**
A splint that keeps the wrist straight is better than one that bends it back.

**Can I keep using my computer?**
Yes, with changes to wrist position, mouse and breaks. See [micro-stretches you can do at your desk](/en/blog/10-chiropractor-backed-micro-stretches-you-can-do-on-your-desk/).
