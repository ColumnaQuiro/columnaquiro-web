**Sleeping on your stomach isn't bad for everyone**, but it's the position that strains the neck most: to breathe, you have to keep your head turned to one side for hours. If you sleep this way and wake up fine, there's no need to change. If you wake with a stiff neck, a headache or an achy lower back, it's worth adjusting the position or gradually changing it.

## What it does to your neck and back

- **Neck**: your head is turned almost as far as it goes. Holding that rotation for hours can leave your neck stiff and sore in the morning, and sometimes with a headache that starts at the base of the skull.
- **Lower back**: on a soft mattress, your pelvis sinks and your lower back arches more than usual.
- **Shoulders and arms**: sleeping with an arm under the pillow or above your head can cause pins and needles in your hand when you wake.

That said, the evidence is thinner than often claimed. A review of studies on sleep posture and spinal pain found few studies, of modest quality, although they suggested that more twisted positions go with more symptoms on waking (Cary et al., *BMJ Open*, 2019). There's no "forbidden" position; there are positions that suit you worse.

## Does it have any benefits?

A few:

- **Snoring and mild sleep apnoea**: face down or on your side, the tongue falls back less than on your back, keeping the airway more open. That's why some people sleep better this way.
- **Falling asleep**: some people simply drop off faster in this position, and that counts too.

What it doesn't do is "stretch your back" or help digestion. If you sleep on your stomach because of snoring, sleeping **on your side** gives the same benefit with less neck twist.

## How to sleep on your stomach with less strain

If you don't want to, or can't, give it up:

- **A very low pillow or none** under your head, so your neck isn't also bent backwards.
- **A thin pillow under your pelvis or stomach**, so your lower back doesn't arch.
- **Semi-prone position** (the "swimmer"): bend the knee on the side you're facing and rest that side of your body on a pillow. It greatly reduces the neck twist.
- **Alternate the side** you turn your head to from night to night.
- **Arms down** by your sides, not above your head.
- **A firm mattress** that doesn't let your pelvis sink.

## Better alternatives

- **On your side**, with a pillow that fills the gap between shoulder and head (so your neck stays straight) and another between your knees. The most balanced option for most people.
- **On your back**, with a low pillow and another under your knees. Good for your back, less so if you snore.

## How to switch position without losing sleep

Your body goes back to its usual position as soon as you fall asleep, so the change is gradual:

1. Start each night in the new position, even if you roll over later.
2. **Hug a long pillow** or place one against your chest: it gives the supported feeling you get face down.
3. Put another pillow behind your back so you don't roll all the way over.
4. Try the semi-prone position first: it's a comfortable halfway step.

Give it a few weeks. If you wake up on your stomach some mornings, that's fine.

## When to see a doctor first

See your doctor if the pain **doesn't change with position** and wakes you at night, comes with fever or weight loss, if you have **weakness** or persistent tingling in your arms, or if your snoring comes with pauses in breathing, gasping or heavy daytime sleepiness (it may be sleep apnoea).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it bad to sleep on your stomach now and then?**
No. Any problem tends to come when it's your position every night and you wake up sore.

**What about during pregnancy?**
As the bump grows it stops being comfortable or practical. From the second trimester, sleeping on your side, preferably the left, is usually recommended.

**And babies?**
Babies should **always sleep on their back**, on a firm surface with no pillows or soft toys: it's the main way to prevent sudden infant death, according to the Spanish Paediatric Association and health services worldwide.

**Which pillow is best if I sleep on my stomach?**
A very thin one, or none. The higher it is, the more your neck bends backwards.

**Can sleeping on your stomach cause headaches?**
It can contribute to headaches that start at the back of the neck. More in our [guide to headaches from the neck](/en/blog/complete-guide-to-treating-cervical-headache/).
