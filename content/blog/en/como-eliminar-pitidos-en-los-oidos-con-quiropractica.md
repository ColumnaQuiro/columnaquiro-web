## Tinnitus has many possible causes

Ringing or buzzing in the ears, known as tinnitus, can stem from many different factors: inner-ear issues, noise exposure, certain medications, or circulatory changes, among others. That's why the first step should always be an evaluation with an ENT specialist or your trusted doctor.

## The connection between the neck and tinnitus

In some people, tinnitus is linked to tension and restrictions in the upper cervical area, where nerve and muscle connections run close to the structures of the ear. In these cases, it's referred to as somatic or cervicogenic tinnitus, and it's often accompanied by neck stiffness or pain at the base of the skull.

## How chiropractic care can help

When that cervical component is identified, gentle adjustments to the upper neck vertebrae, along with releasing the surrounding muscle tension, can help reduce the perceived intensity of the ringing for some people. This isn't about "curing" the ear, but supporting the health of the nervous system connected to that area.

## What else can help

Reducing stress, taking care of neck posture in front of screens, and moderating exposure to loud noise are habits that, combined with chiropractic care, tend to bring overall well-being to people living with this symptom.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does chiropractic care cure tinnitus?**
It doesn't cure it, but in cases with a cervical component, it can help reduce its intensity as part of broader care.

**How do I know if my tinnitus is related to my neck?**
If it's accompanied by neck tension or pain and changes when you move your neck, there may be a connection, though an evaluation from an ear specialist is always necessary.

**Should I see a doctor or a chiropractor first?**
It's best to start with your doctor or an ENT specialist to rule out ear-related causes, and then assess the cervical component.

**How long does it take to notice improvement?**
It varies a lot from person to person; some notice changes within a few weeks of consistent care.

**When should I seek urgent medical attention?**
If the ringing appears suddenly along with hearing loss or intense dizziness, seek emergency medical care right away.
