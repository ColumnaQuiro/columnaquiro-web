Hip pain in young people is **almost never osteoarthritis**. Most often it's overload of the **tendons and muscles** around the hip, a problem with the **shape of the joint** (such as femoroacetabular impingement) or pain coming from the **lower back** or **sacroiliac joint**. The most useful clue is **where it hurts**: the groin, the side or the buttock.

## If it hurts in the groin

It usually comes from the **hip joint** itself:

- **Femoroacetabular impingement**: the shape of the femur or pelvis makes them knock together when you bend the hip. It hurts sitting for a long time, squatting, getting into a car or crossing your legs. Common in young athletes.
- **Labral tear** (the cartilage ring around the joint): pain with clicking or a catching feeling.
- **Hip dysplasia**: a shallow socket, more common in **young women**. It can cause groin pain and a tired hip when walking.
- **Adductor or hip flexor overload**, typical in football, dance or after a big jump in training.

## If it hurts on the side

- **Gluteal tendinopathy** (what used to be called "trochanteric bursitis"): pain over the bone on the side of the hip, worse **lying on that side**, climbing stairs or standing on one leg. More common in **women**.
- **Snapping hip**: a click on the side when walking or turning, usually painless.

## If it hurts in the buttock or at the back

- Pain coming from the **lower back** or the **sacroiliac joint**.
- **Piriformis syndrome** or buttock overload, sometimes with pain down the leg.

## Why does my hip hurt as a young woman?

As well as the above, in young women it's worth considering:

- **Hip dysplasia** and **gluteal tendinopathy**, both more common in women.
- **Pregnancy and the postpartum period**, because of changes in the pelvic ligaments.
- **Endometriosis**, which can cause pelvic, lower back or hip pain that worsens with your period.
- **Femoral neck stress fractures** in runners who train a lot and eat too little for what they burn.

## Other causes to consider

- **Inflamed sacroiliac joints** (spondyloarthritis): buttock or low back pain in under-40s, with **morning stiffness lasting more than 30 minutes** that improves with movement and worsens with rest. A rheumatologist assesses it.
- **Too much sitting**: long hours seated leave the hip stiff and the buttocks weak.

## What helps

- **Adjust your load**: temporarily cut back what triggers it (deep squats, changes of direction, mileage) without stopping moving.
- **Strengthen your buttocks and trunk**: glute bridges, side-lying hip abduction, side planks.
- **Avoid positions that set it off**: crossing your legs, standing with your weight on one leg, sleeping on the painful side (put a pillow between your knees).
- **Get up often** if you sit for long hours.

## When to see a doctor

- Pain after a **fall** that stops you bearing weight.
- **Runners** with groin pain that gets worse when running and comes on earlier each time: possible stress fracture, stop and get it checked.
- **Fever** with hip pain, or a hot, swollen hip.
- Long **morning stiffness** that improves with movement, especially under 40.
- A **limp** or hip or knee pain in children and teenagers.
- Pain that wakes you at night and nothing changes.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it normal for my hip to hurt in my 20s or 30s?**
It's common, and almost never osteoarthritis. Most of the time it's overload or a hip-shape problem that's managed without surgery.

**Can it come from my back?**
Yes. Lower back and sacroiliac pain are often felt in the buttock and hip.

**Can I keep playing sport?**
In most cases yes, adapting whatever triggers the pain.

**Do I need an MRI?**
Not always. It's requested if a labral tear or stress fracture is suspected, or if you're not improving.

**Why does it hurt when I sleep on my side?**
It's usually gluteal tendinopathy. Put a pillow between your knees and avoid sleeping on that side for a while.
