## Nasal breathing vs. mouth breathing: why does it matter?

Breathing through the nose isn't just a matter of habit: the nose filters, humidifies, and warms the air before it reaches the lungs, while also creating a slight resistance that supports better oxygen exchange. Breathing habitually through the mouth, on the other hand, skips these natural filters.

## Effects of habitual mouth breathing

When mouth breathing becomes the usual pattern, especially during sleep, it can be linked to poorer sleep quality, dry mouth and throat, a greater tendency to snore, and, in children, changes in the development of the dental arch and face.

## The connection with posture and jaw development

From a whole-body perspective, the way we breathe influences the position of the head and neck. Mouth breathing tends to go hand in hand with a forward-head posture and a more retracted jaw, which over time can create tension in the neck muscles and throw off the postural balance of the whole spine.

## How to support healthy nasal breathing

Keeping the nasal passages clear, treating allergies or congestion appropriately, and, in children, checking in good time for obstacles such as enlarged adenoids or tonsils, are important steps. Working on head and neck posture can also make nasal breathing more efficient.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it serious to breathe through my mouth occasionally?**
No, it's normal during intense exercise or when there's temporary congestion. The problem arises when it becomes the usual pattern, especially during sleep.

**Does mouth breathing affect children more?**
Yes, because it coincides with key stages of facial and dental development, so a sustained pattern can have a bigger long-term impact.

**Can chiropractic care help with this?**
It can help improve head and neck posture, supporting more efficient breathing mechanics, although any nasal obstruction should be assessed by the appropriate specialist.

**How do I know if I'm breathing poorly at night?**
Waking up with a dry mouth, snoring, or feeling tired despite sleeping enough hours can be signs worth paying attention to.

**When should I see a professional?**
If you suspect you habitually breathe through your mouth, especially in children, or if it's affecting sleep quality or facial development.
