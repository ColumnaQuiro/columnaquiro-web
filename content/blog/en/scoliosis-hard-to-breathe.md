## Important: see a doctor first

If you're noticing breathing difficulty along with scoliosis, the first step is to talk to your doctor, a pulmonologist, or a spine specialist. Breathing difficulty linked to scoliosis can indicate that the curve has reached a degree that affects the rib cage and lung capacity, and that calls for medical evaluation and follow-up that go beyond what chiropractic care alone can offer.

## Why can scoliosis affect breathing?

When a spinal curve is pronounced, it can change the shape of the rib cage, reducing the space the lungs have to expand normally. This usually doesn't happen with milder curves, but moderate to severe curves can limit lung function, especially during physical exertion.

## What medical evaluation usually involves

When breathing symptoms are present, a specialist will typically order imaging to measure the degree of the curve, along with lung function tests to assess breathing capacity. These tests help determine whether close monitoring, bracing, or in some cases a surgical evaluation is needed.

## The role of chiropractic care as complementary support

As part of a care plan supervised by your doctor, chiropractic care can offer complementary support focused on improving mobility of the spine and rib cage, easing associated muscle tension, and supporting better posture. This can contribute to your overall comfort, but it does not treat the breathing difficulty directly and does not replace medical monitoring of the curve.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can chiropractic care cure breathing difficulty caused by scoliosis?**
No. Chiropractic care doesn't treat the underlying cause of the breathing difficulty; its role is to support mobility and comfort, always alongside medical follow-up.

**Does every case of scoliosis affect breathing?**
No, most mild curves don't cause breathing problems. It's usually more pronounced curves that have this effect.

**What should I do if I notice I'm short of breath?**
See your doctor or a pulmonologist as soon as possible so they can assess the degree of the curve and your lung function.

**Can I still get chiropractic adjustments if I have scoliosis?**
In many cases yes, with the technique adapted and coordinated with the rest of your medical team.
