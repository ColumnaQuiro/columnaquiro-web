Yes, in some people the neck plays a part in symptoms felt in the ear: **ringing or buzzing (tinnitus), a blocked feeling, or dizziness when turning the head**. But these symptoms have many possible causes and most of them are in the ear itself, so the first step is always to see your doctor or an ear, nose and throat (ENT) specialist. Only once the ear has been checked does it make sense to look at the neck.

## How the neck and ear are linked

The top of the neck and the ear sit close together and share nerve connections in the brainstem. The neck's muscles and joints also constantly tell the brain where your head is, and that information is combined with signals from the inner ear to keep you balanced. So a very tense or stiff upper neck can:

- **Modulate tinnitus.** This is called *somatic tinnitus*: the ringing changes in volume or pitch when you move your neck, clench your jaw or press on certain muscles.
- **Cause neck-related dizziness**: unsteadiness when turning the head, almost always alongside neck pain or stiffness.
- **Cause a feeling of pressure** around the ear, often together with jaw tension.

## Signs there may be a neck component

- The ringing or dizziness **changes when you move your neck** or jaw.
- It starts or gets worse with **neck pain or stiffness**, or after hours at a screen.
- It began after **whiplash**.
- The ENT specialist found no cause in the ear.

## What treatment can do

For somatic tinnitus, studies of neck treatment are few and of low quality, though some find that reducing neck tension lowers how loud the ringing is (Michiels et al., *Frontiers in Neuroscience*, 2016). The honest version is this: **we don't cure tinnitus and can't "get rid of the ringing"**. Where there is a clear neck component, treating the neck can make the ringing less bothersome and improve dizziness when you turn your head.

At the clinic we check whether your symptoms change with neck and jaw movement. If they do, we work with:

- **Gentle mobilisation and adjustments** at the top of the neck.
- **Soft-tissue work** on the neck and jaw.
- **Exercises** for mobility and head-position control.

If nothing changes within a few sessions, the cause is most likely not your neck, and we'll tell you so.

## What else helps

- Avoiding loud noise and wearing ear protection at concerts or with noisy tools.
- Reducing stress and sleeping well: tinnitus is more noticeable when you're tired or anxious.
- Sound therapy and psychological therapy for tinnitus, arranged by your ENT specialist.

## When to go to A&E

- Sudden hearing loss in one or both ears.
- Ringing that pulses in time with your heartbeat.
- Severe dizziness with vomiting, double vision, trouble speaking or weakness.
- Ringing in one ear only that doesn't go away.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does chiropractic cure tinnitus?**
No. Where there is a clear neck component it can help make it less bothersome, as part of a broader approach.

**How do I know if my tinnitus is related to my neck?**
If it changes when you move your neck or jaw and comes with neck tension, there may be a link. Ear causes need ruling out first.

**Should I see a doctor or a chiropractor first?**
A doctor or ENT specialist. Then, if your ears are fine, we can look at your neck.

**Does all dizziness come from the neck?**
No. Most dizziness has other causes, such as positional vertigo, which is treated with specific manoeuvres. We explain it in [chiropractic for vertigo](/en/blog/chiropractic-for-vertigo/).

**How soon will I notice a change?**
When the cause is in the neck, some change usually shows within a few sessions. If not, there's no point carrying on.
